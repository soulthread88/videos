[01] Ukraine Reveals North Korean POWs Sent to Seoul; Lee Calls Breach ‘Regrettable’. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told the UN General Assembly that two North Korean prisoners of war captured by Ukrainian forces were recently sent to South Korea. A Cheong Wa Dae official said on Sept 25 there had been an understanding “to handle it quietly without disclosure,” and that Ukraine later changed its position. President Lee Jae-myung wrote on X: “I don’t know what circumstances Ukraine had in breaking the confidentiality agreement, but it is very regrettable.” The two were captured in Russia’s Kursk region in January 2025 and entered via a third country.

[02] Choe Son-hui: nuclear resolve ‘absolutely unchanging’ as North rebuffs Seoul, US, Tokyo. North Korean state media put foreign-policy messaging ahead of holiday cheer even on Chuseok, Sept 25. Foreign Minister Choe Son-hui said in a statement that the country’s “firm will to possess nuclear weapons will remain absolutely unchanged even after a hundred or a thousand years.” It is seen as a response to South Korea, the US and Japan reaffirming denuclearization cooperation at the UN General Assembly. State media also prominently featured a letter from Xi Jinping to Kim Jong Un, highlighting North Korea–China friendship.

[03] 119-minute Korea–Mexico summit seals investment treaty after seven years. President Lee Jae-myung and Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum met for 119 minutes, including one-on-one and expanded talks, at the National Palace in Mexico City on Sept 24 local time. They concluded investment treaty negotiations that had dragged on for seven years and adopted a 2026–2030 Joint Action Plan. They also agreed on an AI cooperation declaration, a new space working group and a K-Culture Center in Mexico City. Sheinbaum pledged support for expanded investment by some 560 Korean companies operating in Mexico.

[04] US 10-year yield hits 5.15%, a 19-year high — a risk for KOSPI’s Sept 28 reopening. On Sept 24 local time, the US 10-year Treasury yield topped 5.15% intraday, its highest since 2007, while the 30-year hit 5.446%, the highest since 2004. Brent crude rose more than 3% to above $106 a barrel. Wall Street drifted: the Dow fell 0.31%, the S&P 500 slipped 0.02% and the Nasdaq edged up 0.01%. Losses narrowed sharply on news that the US and Iran were discussing a phased end to the war, including reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Korean markets reopen Monday, Sept 28.

[05] Baseball routed 0-5 by Japan’s amateurs; football crushes Vietnam 4-0. South Korea’s baseball team lost 0-5 on Sept 25 to a Japanese squad made up entirely of amateur company-team players in the first Asian Games Super Round game in Toyohashi, Aichi Prefecture. It is Korea’s first Asian Games loss to Japan in 20 years, since Doha 2006. The lineup managed four hits and hit into three double plays. The same day, the men’s football team beat Vietnam 4-0 behind two goals from Lee Young-jun, reaching the semifinals in its bid for a fourth straight title. The women’s team beat Uzbekistan 6-3.

[06] Parties vie for holiday opinion: ‘tangible results’ vs ‘livelihoods ignored’. Rival parties sent opposing messages on Chuseok Day aimed at holiday sentiment. Democratic Party spokesperson Shin Hyun-young said expanded farm and fishery supplies cut the cost of the ancestral-rite table by 2.3% from last year, and floor spokesperson Lee Joo-hee touted a plan for 1.19 million public housing units by 2030. People Power Party chief spokesperson Park Choong-kwon criticized the government for “consistently showing arrogance and a lack of communication while putting livelihoods on the back burner.” In a Chuseok video message, President Lee said he would “run even harder so that bright light shines evenly on every citizen’s life.”

[07] 6.84M cars on the road on Chuseok Day; return traffic eases by early Sept 26. Korea Expressway Corp. expected 6.84 million vehicles on the nation’s expressways on Sept 25, Chuseok Day. It forecast congestion toward Seoul peaking at 3–4 p.m. and easing around 2–3 a.m. on Sept 26. For departures from tollgates at 5 p.m., trips to Seoul took 6 hours 40 minutes from Busan, 5 hours 50 minutes from Gwangju, 5 hours 40 minutes from Daegu and 3 hours 50 minutes from Daejeon. Expressway tolls are waived from Sept 24 until midnight Sept 27.

[08] Chuseok fire and blast at Yangju villa: 1 dead, 6 hurt including police. A fire broke out around 1:27 p.m. on Sept 25, Chuseok Day, on the third floor of a low-rise apartment building in Deokgye-dong, Yangju, Gyeonggi Province. Police had been dispatched after a report that “a drunk man is spraying gasoline.” One man died at the scene, and an explosion during firefighting injured six people: four police officers, one firefighter and one resident. Fire authorities deployed 74 personnel and 34 pieces of equipment, brought the main blaze under control around 2:19 p.m. and rescued two officers trapped on the roof.

[09] Cloudy, rainy Chuseok nationwide; the holiday full moon hard to see. Skies were mostly cloudy nationwide on Sept 25, Chuseok Day, with light rain in parts of the central and southern regions from late afternoon. Morning lows were forecast at 14–21 C and daytime highs at 22–28 C. The Chuseok full moon reached its highest point at 11:31 p.m., but heavy clouds made it hard to see in most areas. Inland central regions are expected to see wide daily temperature swings of 10–15 degrees for some time.

[10] Hur Jin-ho’s ‘Assassin(s)’ tops the Chuseok box office for a second straight day. Director Hur Jin-ho’s film “Assassin(s)” drew 288,485 viewers on Sept 24, the first day of the Chuseok holiday, holding the No. 1 box-office spot for a second day. Since opening Sept 23, it has drawn 447,073 viewers, with advance bookings above 300,000. Starring Yoo Hae-jin, Park Hae-il and Lee Min-ho, it explores the background of the Aug. 15, 1974, shooting of the first lady. “Tazza 4: Song of Beelzebub” was second.

[11] When ‘quiet diplomacy’ over POW transfers breaks down. South Korea and Ukraine had agreed to handle the North Korean POW transfer confidentially, but it was disclosed first on the UN stage. Quiet diplomacy is a safeguard for the individuals’ safety and for transit through third countries. If backlash from North Korea and Russia grows after the disclosure, transferring other prisoners held on the Russian side could become harder. Going forward, agreements need to spell out the scope and timing of any disclosure.

[12] An ‘unchanging’ nuclear pledge and a narrowing door to dialogue. North Korea’s Rodong Sinmun ran Foreign Minister Choe Son-hui’s statement on page 2 on Chuseok Day, declaring nuclear-state status an eternal principle. Page 1 carried Xi Jinping’s letter, showcasing close North Korea–China ties. The more Seoul, Washington and Tokyo stress denuclearization cooperation, the more Pyongyang appears to counter with nuclear deterrence and the China card. For now, managing tension, not dialogue, is the task.

[13] Record September exports, half of them chips. Exports for Sept 1–20 reached $71.4 billion, up 78.3% from a year earlier and a record for the period. Semiconductors surged 259.4% to $34.1 billion, 47.8% of the total. When a single product carries half of exports, the shock is just as large when the chip cycle turns. On the first trading day after the holiday, US rates and chip stocks are the variables.

[14] Chip plants that never stop, even for the holidays. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix chip plants ran on four-team, three-shift rotations through the Chuseok holiday. Stopping a line ruins products in process, and recalibrating temperature and chemical concentrations takes a long time. August chip exports hit a monthly record of $46.65 billion, yet SK hynix shares fell more than 27% from July through Sept 23. Doubts over whether AI investment will translate into real memory demand explain the gap.

[15] 6.84 million cars on the move and 416 emergency rooms. Expressways were expected to carry 6.84 million vehicles on Chuseok Day, and rain added to the holiday’s worst congestion. The same day, 416 emergency rooms stayed open 24 hours, and more than 5,000 clinics and pharmacies operated. During mass travel, when accidents and sudden illness spike, the core of the safety net is sending mild symptoms to open local clinics and serious cases to 119, Korea’s emergency number.

[16] Ganggangsullae: a circle of joined hands under the full moon. Ganggangsullae is a group game of song and dance by women, traditionally performed on moonlit nights around Chuseok in the coastal areas of South Jeolla Province. Dancers circle, then move into games such as bracken-picking, herring-weaving and tile-stepping. Legend holds that during the Battle of Myeongnyang, women circled a mountain to make the Joseon army look larger. It was inscribed on UNESCO’s Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2009, and the National Folk Museum of Korea holds performances and hands-on sessions on Sept 26–27.

[17] “The moon is roundest on Chuseok night”? Not true. Every year the claim circulates that the Chuseok moon is the year’s ‘perfect full moon.’ According to the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute, this year’s Chuseok moon rose at 5:34 p.m. in Seoul and reached its highest point at 11:31 p.m. But the moment the moon is truly full comes two days later, at 1:49 a.m. on Sept 27. Because the timing of the 15th lunar day and the full moon varies each year, the claim that it is roundest on Chuseok is not true.

[18] 1983: the ‘wait’ that prevented nuclear war. On Sept 26, 1983, a Soviet early-warning satellite signaled five intercontinental ballistic missiles incoming from the United States. The duty officer, Lt. Col. Stanislav Petrov, suspected a malfunction, reasoning that a real first strike would involve hundreds of missiles, and held off reporting it. Indeed, ground radar detected nothing. With North Korea reaffirming its nuclear resolve on Chuseok Day, it is a reminder of the weight of a human’s final judgment.

[19] Jongmyo without a reservation: last chance this holiday. Jongmyo, the shrine housing the spirit tablets of Joseon kings and queens, normally requires reservations, but during the Chuseok holiday, Sept 24–27, entry is free with no booking. It is open 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., with last entry at 5 p.m. Highlights are the 101-meter-long Jeongjeon main hall and Yeongnyeongjeon. Jongmyo became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995, and Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung and Changgyeonggung palaces are also free during the same period.

[20] 17-year-old Hwang Seo-hyeon wins first balance beam gold in 40 years. Hwang Seo-hyeon of Incheon Physical Education High School won the women’s balance beam final in artistic gymnastics at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games on Sept 25 with 14.700 points. She edged China’s Qiu Qiyuan by 0.134 points, while Japan’s Mana Okamura took bronze. It is the first time in 40 years a Korean has won the event, since Seo Sun-aeng at Seoul 1986. Rising from fifth in qualifying to gold, Hwang said, “I want to be remembered as an athlete who always smiles brightly.”

[22] Iran: 7-day Hormuz reopening plan starts as soon as US accepts. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said in New York on Sept 25, “If the United States accepts this plan tomorrow, the seven days start tomorrow.” Under the sequence, once the US takes steps such as lifting the blockade, Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz on day six and resume negotiations on day seven. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the contacts were productive but it was too early to call it a major breakthrough.
