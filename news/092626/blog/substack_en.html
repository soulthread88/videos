01. Ukraine Reveals North Korean POWs Sent to Seoul; Lee Calls Breach ‘Regrettable’

SECURITY · DIPLOMACY

UN General Assembly hall (file photo) — Photo: Basil D Soufi · Wikimedia Commons

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told the UN General Assembly that two North Korean prisoners of war captured by Ukrainian forces were recently sent to South Korea. A Cheong Wa Dae official said on Sept 25 there had been an understanding “to handle it quietly without disclosure,” and that Ukraine later changed its position. President Lee Jae-myung wrote on X: “I don’t know what circumstances Ukraine had in breaking the confidentiality agreement, but it is very regrettable.” The two were captured in Russia’s Kursk region in January 2025 and entered via a third country.

📘 Prisoner of war (POW): a soldier captured by the enemy during hostilities. The Third Geneva Convention guarantees humane treatment and repatriation procedures.

PRO ✔ Cheong Wa Dae, the presidential office, maintains that confidentiality was right because quiet handling was the fastest, safest way to transfer the prisoners.

CON ✖ With the understanding broken, concerns are growing over North Korean backlash and diplomatic strain, and that talks on remaining prisoners have become harder.

Source: Kyunghyang Shinmun

02. Choe Son-hui: nuclear resolve ‘absolutely unchanging’ as North rebuffs Seoul, US, Tokyo

NORTH KOREA

Panmunjom (file photo) — Photo: Christophe95 · Wikimedia Commons

North Korean state media put foreign-policy messaging ahead of holiday cheer even on Chuseok, Sept 25. Foreign Minister Choe Son-hui said in a statement that the country’s “firm will to possess nuclear weapons will remain absolutely unchanged even after a hundred or a thousand years.” It is seen as a response to South Korea, the US and Japan reaffirming denuclearization cooperation at the UN General Assembly. State media also prominently featured a letter from Xi Jinping to Kim Jong Un, highlighting North Korea–China friendship.

📘 Denuclearization: dismantling nuclear weapons and related facilities and materials to eliminate nuclear capability — a shared goal of South Korea, the US and Japan.

PRO ✔ Some read the North’s fierce reaction as proof that trilateral cooperation is applying pressure.

CON ✖ By making nuclear status a non-negotiable premise, the North has further narrowed room for renewed dialogue, analysts say.

Source: Segye Ilbo

03. 119-minute Korea–Mexico summit seals investment treaty after seven years

DIPLOMACY · TRADE

Mexico’s National Palace (file photo) — Photo: Drkgk · Wikimedia Commons

President Lee Jae-myung and Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum met for 119 minutes, including one-on-one and expanded talks, at the National Palace in Mexico City on Sept 24 local time. They concluded investment treaty negotiations that had dragged on for seven years and adopted a 2026–2030 Joint Action Plan. They also agreed on an AI cooperation declaration, a new space working group and a K-Culture Center in Mexico City. Sheinbaum pledged support for expanded investment by some 560 Korean companies operating in Mexico.

📘 Bilateral investment treaty (BIT): a two-country treaty protecting investors’ assets from expropriation and discrimination and setting dispute-settlement procedures.

PRO ✔ It secures an institutional safety net with Mexico, a North American production base, amid US tariff barriers.

CON ✖ Critics note the deals are mostly memorandums of understanding, so it is unclear whether they will lead to real investment or a trade agreement.

Source: Etoday

04. US 10-year yield hits 5.15%, a 19-year high — a risk for KOSPI’s Sept 28 reopening

ECONOMY

New York Stock Exchange (file photo) — Photo: Arild Vågen · Wikimedia Commons

On Sept 24 local time, the US 10-year Treasury yield topped 5.15% intraday, its highest since 2007, while the 30-year hit 5.446%, the highest since 2004. Brent crude rose more than 3% to above $106 a barrel. Wall Street drifted: the Dow fell 0.31%, the S&P 500 slipped 0.02% and the Nasdaq edged up 0.01%. Losses narrowed sharply on news that the US and Iran were discussing a phased end to the war, including reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Korean markets reopen Monday, Sept 28.

📘 10-year Treasury yield: the return on 10-year US government bonds, a benchmark for loan and asset prices worldwide.

PRO ✔ Progress in Hormuz talks could stabilize oil prices, good news for energy importer Korea.

CON ✖ High rates combined with high oil could weigh on Korean stocks and the won on the first trading day after the holiday.

Source: Money Today

05. Baseball routed 0-5 by Japan’s amateurs; football crushes Vietnam 4-0

SPORTS

Baseball stadium (file photo) — Photo: Degueulasse · Wikimedia Commons

South Korea’s baseball team lost 0-5 on Sept 25 to a Japanese squad made up entirely of amateur company-team players in the first Asian Games Super Round game in Toyohashi, Aichi Prefecture. It is Korea’s first Asian Games loss to Japan in 20 years, since Doha 2006. The lineup managed four hits and hit into three double plays. The same day, the men’s football team beat Vietnam 4-0 behind two goals from Lee Young-jun, reaching the semifinals in its bid for a fourth straight title. The women’s team beat Uzbekistan 6-3.

📘 Super Round: a second-stage round in which top group-stage teams play again to decide the finalists.

PRO ✔ The men’s football team’s rout, led by first-time starter Lee Young-jun’s two goals, bodes well for a fourth consecutive gold.

CON ✖ Criticism of competitiveness is mounting after a team of professionals was shut out by amateurs.

Source: Newsis

06. Parties vie for holiday opinion: ‘tangible results’ vs ‘livelihoods ignored’

POLITICS

National Assembly (file photo) — Photo: Cjb8293 · Wikimedia Commons

Rival parties sent opposing messages on Chuseok Day aimed at holiday sentiment. Democratic Party spokesperson Shin Hyun-young said expanded farm and fishery supplies cut the cost of the ancestral-rite table by 2.3% from last year, and floor spokesperson Lee Joo-hee touted a plan for 1.19 million public housing units by 2030. People Power Party chief spokesperson Park Choong-kwon criticized the government for “consistently showing arrogance and a lack of communication while putting livelihoods on the back burner.” In a Chuseok video message, President Lee said he would “run even harder so that bright light shines evenly on every citizen’s life.”

📘 Dinner-table sentiment: public opinion shaped by political and economic talk when families gather for the holidays.

PRO ✔ The Democratic Party pointed to lower holiday-table costs and large-scale housing supply as results for livelihoods.

CON ✖ The People Power Party said high prices and a slowing economy have increased household burdens and demanded a governance overhaul.

Source: Aju Business Daily

07. 6.84M cars on the road on Chuseok Day; return traffic eases by early Sept 26

SOCIETY

Gyeongbu Expressway (file photo) — Photo: Trainholic · Wikimedia Commons

Korea Expressway Corp. expected 6.84 million vehicles on the nation’s expressways on Sept 25, Chuseok Day. It forecast congestion toward Seoul peaking at 3–4 p.m. and easing around 2–3 a.m. on Sept 26. For departures from tollgates at 5 p.m., trips to Seoul took 6 hours 40 minutes from Busan, 5 hours 50 minutes from Gwangju, 5 hours 40 minutes from Daegu and 3 hours 50 minutes from Daejeon. Expressway tolls are waived from Sept 24 until midnight Sept 27.

📘 Gwigyeong: returning to Seoul and the capital region from one’s hometown after the holiday.

PRO ✔ Toll waivers cut holiday travel costs and ease household burdens.

CON ✖ Critics say waivers increase car travel, worsen congestion and cost the expressway operator revenue.

Source: Seoul Shinmun

08. Chuseok fire and blast at Yangju villa: 1 dead, 6 hurt including police

SAFETY

Fire truck (file photo) — Photo: LoMit · Wikimedia Commons

A fire broke out around 1:27 p.m. on Sept 25, Chuseok Day, on the third floor of a low-rise apartment building in Deokgye-dong, Yangju, Gyeonggi Province. Police had been dispatched after a report that “a drunk man is spraying gasoline.” One man died at the scene, and an explosion during firefighting injured six people: four police officers, one firefighter and one resident. Fire authorities deployed 74 personnel and 34 pieces of equipment, brought the main blaze under control around 2:19 p.m. and rescued two officers trapped on the roof.

📘 Initial suppression: the stage when a fire is no longer at risk of spreading, before it is fully extinguished.

PRO ✔ Firefighters and police responded quickly, controlling the main blaze in about 50 minutes and rescuing those trapped.

CON ✖ With five responders injured by the blast, some say on-site safety rules for flammable-substance calls need review.

Source: Seoul Shinmun

09. Cloudy, rainy Chuseok nationwide; the holiday full moon hard to see

WEATHER

Full moon (file photo) — Photo: Giles Laurent · Wikimedia Commons

Skies were mostly cloudy nationwide on Sept 25, Chuseok Day, with light rain in parts of the central and southern regions from late afternoon. Morning lows were forecast at 14–21 C and daytime highs at 22–28 C. The Chuseok full moon reached its highest point at 11:31 p.m., but heavy clouds made it hard to see in most areas. Inland central regions are expected to see wide daily temperature swings of 10–15 degrees for some time.

📘 Culmination: the moment a celestial body crosses due south and reaches its highest point in the sky for the day.

PRO ✔ Cool temperatures and light rain caused little inconvenience for grave visits and travel.

CON ✖ Clouds and rain spoiled the chance to see the full moon, and wide temperature swings call for health precautions.

10. Hur Jin-ho’s ‘Assassin(s)’ tops the Chuseok box office for a second straight day

CULTURE

Movie theater (file photo) — Photo: Rodhullandemu · Wikimedia Commons

Director Hur Jin-ho’s film “Assassin(s)” drew 288,485 viewers on Sept 24, the first day of the Chuseok holiday, holding the No. 1 box-office spot for a second day. Since opening Sept 23, it has drawn 447,073 viewers, with advance bookings above 300,000. Starring Yoo Hae-jin, Park Hae-il and Lee Min-ho, it explores the background of the Aug. 15, 1974, shooting of the first lady. “Tazza 4: Song of Beelzebub” was second.

📘 Box office: a film’s ticket sales and ranking. Korea uses the Korean Film Council’s integrated ticketing network as the benchmark.

PRO ✔ Praise continues for a faster pace than typical holiday hits and for the actors’ performances.

CON ✖ These are early-holiday numbers; it remains to be seen whether word of mouth sustains a long run.

When ‘quiet diplomacy’ over POW transfers breaks down

DIPLOMACY · SECURITY

Airport (file photo) — Photo: Sgroey · Wikimedia Commons

South Korea and Ukraine had agreed to handle the North Korean POW transfer confidentially, but it was disclosed first on the UN stage. Quiet diplomacy is a safeguard for the individuals’ safety and for transit through third countries. If backlash from North Korea and Russia grows after the disclosure, transferring other prisoners held on the Russian side could become harder. Going forward, agreements need to spell out the scope and timing of any disclosure.

📘 Non-refoulement: the international-law principle that no one should be returned to a place where they risk persecution.

The more Seoul, Washington and Tokyo stress denuclearization cooperation, the more Pyongyang appears to counter with nuclear deterrence and the China card. 📘 Nuclear deterrence: using nuclear weapons to discourage an adversary from attacking.

ECONOMY

Chip plants that never stop, even for the holidays

INDUSTRY

Chip fabrication (file photo) — Photo: Aileen Devlin/Jefferson Lab from Newport News USA · Wikimedia Commons

Samsung Electronics and SK hynix chip plants ran on four-team, three-shift rotations through the Chuseok holiday. Stopping a line ruins products in process, and recalibrating temperature and chemical concentrations takes a long time. August chip exports hit a monthly record of $46.65 billion, yet SK hynix shares fell more than 27% from July through Sept 23. Doubts over whether AI investment will translate into real memory demand explain the gap.

📘 HBM: high-bandwidth memory for AI that stacks DRAM vertically to sharply boost data-transfer speed.

Ganggangsullae: a circle of joined hands under the full moon

TRADITION · CHUSEOK

Ganggangsullae (file photo) — Photo: Korea.net / Korean Culture and Information Service (Jeon Han) · Wikimedia Commons

Ganggangsullae is a group game of song and dance by women, traditionally performed on moonlit nights around Chuseok in the coastal areas of South Jeolla Province. Dancers circle, then move into games such as bracken-picking, herring-weaving and tile-stepping. Legend holds that during the Battle of Myeongnyang, women circled a mountain to make the Joseon army look larger. It was inscribed on UNESCO’s Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2009, and the National Folk Museum of Korea holds performances and hands-on sessions on Sept 26–27.

📘 Intangible Cultural Heritage: performing arts, games, rituals and other intangible culture listed by UNESCO for their value in being passed down.

PRO ✔ Anyone can join just by taking a hand, making it a holiday game that brings generations together.

CON ✖ Today it is more often seen in museum performances than played by villages themselves, raising questions about how it is passed down.

Source: Asia Economy

“The moon is roundest on Chuseok night”? Not true

FACT CHECK · FULL MOON

Full moon (file photo) — Photo: Basile Morin · Wikimedia Commons

Every year the claim circulates that the Chuseok moon is the year’s ‘perfect full moon.’ According to the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute, this year’s Chuseok moon rose at 5:34 p.m. in Seoul and reached its highest point at 11:31 p.m. But the moment the moon is truly full comes two days later, at 1:49 a.m. on Sept 27. Because the timing of the 15th lunar day and the full moon varies each year, the claim that it is roundest on Chuseok is not true.

📘 Full moon (mang): the moment the sun, Earth and moon align and the moon is at its fullest.

PRO ✔ Around the 15th lunar day the moon looks nearly full to the naked eye, so as everyday speech it is not wrong.

CON ✖ Astronomically, the roundest moment varies each year, so equating it with Chuseok Day is incorrect.

Source: Seoul Shinmun

1983: the ‘wait’ that prevented nuclear war

HISTORY · SEPTEMBER 26

Radar (file photo) — Photo: Sims Gregory · Wikimedia Commons

On Sept 26, 1983, a Soviet early-warning satellite signaled five intercontinental ballistic missiles incoming from the United States. The duty officer, Lt. Col. Stanislav Petrov, suspected a malfunction, reasoning that a real first strike would involve hundreds of missiles, and held off reporting it. Indeed, ground radar detected nothing. With North Korea reaffirming its nuclear resolve on Chuseok Day, it is a reminder of the weight of a human’s final judgment.

📘 Early-warning system: a network of satellites and radar that detects enemy missile launches early to buy time to respond.

PRO ✔ It shows how a human final check can prevent catastrophe caused by machine error.

CON ✖ Others argue that relying on one person’s instinct to avert a crisis instead exposed the system’s fragility.

Source: Wikipedia

Jongmyo without a reservation: last chance this holiday

HIDDEN KOREA · HOLIDAY WALK

Jongmyo Jeongjeon (file photo) — Photo: Cultural Heritage Administration · Wikimedia Commons

Jongmyo, the shrine housing the spirit tablets of Joseon kings and queens, normally requires reservations, but during the Chuseok holiday, Sept 24–27, entry is free with no booking. It is open 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., with last entry at 5 p.m. Highlights are the 101-meter-long Jeongjeon main hall and Yeongnyeongjeon. Jongmyo became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995, and Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung and Changgyeonggung palaces are also free during the same period.

📘 Sinju (spirit tablet): a wooden tablet bearing a deceased person’s name, honored in ancestral rites. Jongmyo is the shrine that houses them.

PRO ✔ Visitors can take in quiet World Heritage architecture without the usual booking hassle.

CON ✖ Free admission may draw crowds, and no entry is allowed after 5 p.m.

Source: Seoul Shinmun

17-year-old Hwang Seo-hyeon wins first balance beam gold in 40 years

SPOTLIGHT · ASIAN GAMES

Artistic gymnastics balance beam (file photo) — Photo: Fernando Frazão/Agência Brasil · Wikimedia Commons

Hwang Seo-hyeon of Incheon Physical Education High School won the women’s balance beam final in artistic gymnastics at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games on Sept 25 with 14.700 points. She edged China’s Qiu Qiyuan by 0.134 points, while Japan’s Mana Okamura took bronze. It is the first time in 40 years a Korean has won the event, since Seo Sun-aeng at Seoul 1986. Rising from fifth in qualifying to gold, Hwang said, “I want to be remembered as an athlete who always smiles brightly.”

📘 Balance beam: a women’s artistic gymnastics event performed on a 10 cm-wide beam, ranked on combined difficulty and execution scores.

PRO ✔ A teenager’s breakthrough adds momentum to a generational shift in Korean women’s gymnastics.

CON ✖ The narrow 0.134-point margin means difficulty must rise further to compete with China and Japan.

Source: Segye Ilbo

US proposes US–Ukraine–Russia working-level talks in the UAE

WORLD · UKRAINE

Meeting room (file photo) — Photo: Wadiia · Wikimedia Commons

Zelenskyy said on Sept 25 that the United States proposed holding trilateral US–Ukraine–Russia working-level talks in the United Arab Emirates. He said negotiators would “return within 10 days with new prospects or results.” Trilateral talks were held earlier this year, but a fourth round in March was postponed due to the situation in the Middle East. The same day, Ukraine struck Russian oil refineries.

📘 Working-level talks: meetings of negotiating teams, not leaders, to first settle details such as ceasefire terms.

PRO ✔ A stalled negotiating framework revived after six months could speed up ceasefire talks.

CON ✖ With strikes continuing even as talks are prepared, it may prove diplomacy that only buys time.

Source: Kyiv Post

Iran: 7-day Hormuz reopening plan starts as soon as US accepts

WORLD · MIDDLE EAST

Oil tanker (file photo) — Photo: U.S. Navy photo by Petty Officer 3rd Class Janae Chambers · Wikimedia Commons

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said in New York on Sept 25, “If the United States accepts this plan tomorrow, the seven days start tomorrow.” Under the sequence, once the US takes steps such as lifting the blockade, Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz on day six and resume negotiations on day seven. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the contacts were productive but it was too early to call it a major breakthrough.

📘 Strait of Hormuz: a narrow waterway linking the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, a key route for global oil shipments.

PRO ✔ Iran’s concrete timetable offers a thread toward reopening the strait and stabilizing oil prices.

CON ✖ With the US seeing sanctions as negotiating leverage, it may amount to little more than an Iranian pressure tactic.

Source: ANI

Wall Street rises despite Treasury yields above 5.2%

WORLD · US ECONOMY

New York financial district (file photo) — Photo: Dietmar Rabich · Wikimedia Commons

On Sept 25, the US 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.225%, its highest since 2007, and the 30-year hit 5.502%, the highest since 2004. Markets put the odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike in October at 66%. Even so, strength in Big Tech lifted the Dow 0.93% to close at 51,828.62 and the S&P 500 0.51% to 7,743.41.

📘 Treasury yield: the interest rate on government borrowing, a benchmark for mortgage and corporate loan rates.

PRO ✔ Stocks holding up despite high rates signals that AI investment and corporate earnings are supporting the economy.

CON ✖ If 19-year-high rates persist, debt burdens on households and businesses and the risk of a market correction will grow.

Source: TheStreet

US–China tariff truce extended to January

WORLD · US–CHINA

Container ship (file photo) — Photo: Muhammad Mahdi Karim · Wikimedia Commons

Xi Jinping’s state visit to the United States ended on Sept 25, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the two countries pushed the tariff truce’s expiry from Nov 10 to January 2027. They also agreed to create a trade council to designate goods shielded from disputes and an AI safety communication channel. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer called the channel “something like a red phone.”

📘 Tariff truce: an agreement by two countries to pause additional tariffs for a set period and keep negotiating.

PRO ✔ It prevents a renewed tariff war and creates the first crisis-communication channel on AI.

CON ✖ Observers say substantive agreements were few and core issues such as Taiwan were put off.

Source: CBS News

Anthropic signs $11.6 billion cloud deal with Akamai

WORLD · AI · TECH

Data center (file photo) — Photo: Rsparks3 · Wikimedia Commons

AI company Anthropic agreed to use $11.6 billion of Akamai’s cloud infrastructure over seven years. With additional commitments, the deal could grow to about $20 billion. Akamai granted Anthropic the right to buy up to 5% of its shares, and Akamai stock rose 15% intraday on Sept 25. However, Akamai must first invest about $5.5 billion in facilities before revenue comes in.

📘 Warrant: the right to buy a company’s shares at a set price.

PRO ✔ AI demand spreading beyond GPUs to general cloud infrastructure opens new growth opportunities.

CON ✖ The heavy upfront investment could strain cash flow if AI demand slows.

Source: TechCrunch

Fighting resumes in Ethiopia’s Tigray; communications cut

WORLD · AFRICA

Ethiopian highlands (file photo) — Photo: Indrik myneur · Wikimedia Commons

Internet and phone service were cut on Sept 25 as fighting resumed between federal forces and Tigrayan forces in northern Ethiopia’s Tigray region. The Tigray People’s Liberation Front had earlier seized three airports, and Ethiopian Airlines canceled all flights to Tigray. An aid worker said the blackout, coming amid an eight-month cash shortage, is “devastating for everyone.”

📘 TPLF: a Tigray-based political and armed movement that fought a civil war with Ethiopia’s government in 2020–2022.

PRO ✔ Swift international intervention could still prevent a catastrophe like the last civil war.

CON ✖ A communications blackout hides the extent of the damage and blocks aid, risking a renewed famine crisis.
